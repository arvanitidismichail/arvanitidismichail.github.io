Dr Michail Arvanitidis (PT, PhD, MSc, MMACP) is a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at the Centre of Precision Rehabilitation for Spinal Pain (CPR Spine), School of Sport, Exercise and Rehabilitation Sciences, University of Birmingham, United Kingdom. His current research examines cervical adaptations in NASA astronauts following spaceflight, aiming to enhance astronaut health for future missions. His main interests involve studying motor adaptations in pain-affected or vulnerable populations.

Research

Spaceflight and the cervical spine

Astronauts return from spaceflight at markedly higher risk of cervical disc herniation, and the mechanisms are largely unexplored. Within the international Cervical in Space trial, funded by the UK Space Agency, I examine how the cervical spine and its musculature adapt after flight, and what that means for countermeasure design.

Motor adaptations in pain

How persistent musculoskeletal pain reshapes force control, motor unit discharge and the spatial organisation of muscle activity — across the trunk, neck, knee and ankle, and in populations made vulnerable by ageing or injury.

Methods and biofeedback

Building the measurement side of that work: high-density surface EMG analysis pipelines, simultaneous EMG and ultrasound recording through transparent electrode grids, and real-time augmented-reality biofeedback for retraining muscle activation.
